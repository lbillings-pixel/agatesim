| **M7: Specimen analysis I** | Photo scale with perspective correction; automatic specimen outline; observed fields; form identification validated on synthetic photos (passes reliable, fails uninformative); replica fitted in the specimen's outline; Specimens screen ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-specimen-analysis-first-pass.md)). | **M8: Mechanistic models** | Front feedback (H1): self-organised bands verify fortification and seam at the mechanistic tier ([ADR 0013](adr/0013-mechanistic-front-feedback.md)); supply forcing and band-sequence statistics: bands record the forcing's level, not its character ([ADR 0014](adr/0014-band-sequences-as-records.md)); Liesegang precipitation (H2) on an array backend: closed rings with the Liesegang spacing law, but rounded corners, so H2 fails the fortification corner test ([ADR 0015](adr/0015-mechanistic-liesegang.md)); CuPy backend with a fused CUDA kernel (about 70× the CPU); hindered settling (H4) verifies waterline ([ADR 0016](adr/0016-mechanistic-settling.md)); H1 with explicit diffusion: exact linear stability finds no oscillation in 4,000 parameter sets (earlier stick-slip cycles were discretisation artefacts), so H1's bands rest on the boundary-layer approximation. A surface state (adsorbed impurity) restores a converged 1D oscillation, but its fronts are unstable to ripples at all short wavelengths, so the 2D corner test has no converged answer without a further smoothing mechanism (backlog) ([ADR 0017](adr/0017-h1-without-the-boundary-layer.md)); later: H1 and H4 chained as stages verify the combined form ([ADR 0020](adr/0020-staged-mechanisms.md)) | done |
| **M9: 3D** | Cavities as solids (8 presets, drawn outlines extruded); procedural fortification and waterline, H1, H4 and the H1 + H4 stages evaluated at any point; cuts as ordinary 2D results, judged by the unchanged criteria; random-cut surveys: layers and the combined form pass over 90% of cuts, fortification 24–30% because band corners drift off the wall corner's bisector on oblique cuts, 71–76% once the corner test also looks at each band's own corner ([ADR 0022](adr/0022-corners-at-the-bands-own-corner.md)); apparent band thickness 1.07–1.18× true; 3D screen with a rotatable WebGL2 view opened at the cut ([ADR 0021](adr/0021-three-dimensional-runs.md)) | done |
| **M10: Specimen analysis II** | Discriminants between mechanisms (corners, ring closure, floor-width law), validated on simulations; through photos only ring closure survives ([ADR 0018](adr/0018-mechanism-discriminants.md)); hand-marked band boundaries as ground truth for real photos, scored against chance; a full-resolution boundary detector finds about twice as many marks as chance, near the limit of the current photos ([ADR 0019](adr/0019-hand-marked-ground-truth.md)); waiting for better photos: tracing-independent corners, layer tracing, simulation-based inference with uncertainty, quartz and inclusion detection | paused; resumed in stages as M30-M35 (ADR 0063) |
| **M11: Growth from nuclei in 3D** | The procedural growth model in 3D with exact solids between stages (wall offset, spherulite balls, settled bed); sites on the growth surface from the fixed reference lattice; site ownership propagated inward (geometric selection); eye, botryoidal and quartz-geode 3D presets. Eyes become closed bullseyes on cuts near the wall; random cuts: botryoidal 98%, eye 11% (only cuts near a nucleation site root the eye on the wall), geode 7% (quartz coarsens inward in 3D, Spearman 0.86, but a cut slices crystals at random) ([ADR 0023](adr/0023-growth-from-nuclei-in-3d.md)); with eye and geode tests that survive the cut, 30% and 76% ([ADR 0024](adr/0024-eye-and-geode-tests-on-cuts.md)) | done |
| **M12: Inclusions in 3D** | Filaments as exact solid pieces lined by later growth (tube agate: rings on cuts within about 30° of square-on to the filaments, stripes along them); multi-particle aggregation on a cubic lattice for moss and plume (all random cuts pass moss; plume passes cuts that contain gravity). Against Hou et al. 2023: the length law matches at 10% particle concentration, the 3D dimension only where dendrites merge ([ADR 0025](adr/0025-inclusions-in-3d.md)) | done |
| **M13: Growth animation** | Play, pause, speeds from 0.25× to 4× and looping on every slab view's growth slider (2D runs and previews); on the 3D screen one control drives both the cut and the rotatable view, which shows the growth front advancing through the solid (each voxel's deposition time sent with its colour). Owner request, 2026-10-16. Then: the slider names the stage forming (wall lining, spherulites, quartz crystals, settled layers, branched inclusions, H1 and H4 stages), and the animation saves as an animated GIF (encoded in the browser) or a video (MP4 where the browser records it, else WebM), with a caption strip; the 3D screen saves the 3D view and the cut side by side | done |
| **M14: H2 in 3D and the corner benchmark** | Owner request: higher-resolution reference photos for the corner question. Five polyhedral agates at 4,700-7,300 px (CC BY 4.0, fetched on demand); measured on all five: three are as sharp as the photos resolve (0.03-0.4 band spacings, like H1 and procedural lining at 4096 px), two round moderately (0.63, and arcs at a very obtuse corner), none as much as H2 (1.6-2.4) ([ADR 0026](adr/0026-corner-benchmark-from-high-resolution-photos.md)). H2 in 3D on the GPU (38 million cells, 66 s): no random cut passes as fortification (H1: 71%), as rings break into spots and corners round more (median 0.66 against H1's 0.38) ([ADR 0027](adr/0027-liesegang-in-3d.md)) | done |
| **M15: A regularised H1 at the corner** | Owner request: can H1 round corners a little, like two of the five photos? Literature: capillarity (Gibbs–Thomson) is the classic smoothing of a growth front, and the Wang–Merino agate model uses it, with fibres as the unstable bumps. Exact analysis: surface diffusion of the adsorbed coverage never stops short ripples; a curvature term always does above a critical value, without changing the 1D rhythm. A rebuilt 2D GPU solver (checked against a 1D solver and the exact ripple rates) puts the front in a corner: the mitres are sharp to moderately rounded by the photo measure (0.16–0.53 band spacings, like the photos), but the corner lags (its tip moves at 74–81% of the speed a sharp corner needs), so bands thin toward corners and drift out of phase, crossing at low angles. Below the critical value the front turns cellular ([ADR 0028](adr/0028-regularised-h1-at-the-corner.md)) | done |
| **M16: Do bands thin toward corners?** | The regularised H1 predicts bands thin toward corners (0.68–0.78 of mid-face a quarter of the way along). A marker band that rings the polygon is measured along the faces of three reference photos (orange bands in n02 and n04 picked out by colour; a milky band in the polygonal agate picked out by brightness, with the lighting evened out), with the hand marks saved in a file: median 1.02 over 22 half-faces (95% interval 0.92–1.11), so the bands mostly keep their thickness, as corners that keep up predict ([ADR 0029](adr/0029-bands-toward-corners-in-real-agates.md)) | done (three photos; preliminary) |
| **M17: H1 with weak silica depletion** | Every oscillating H1 set so far starved its front of silica, which is what makes corners lag. A scan with low silica uptake finds sets that oscillate with silica at 92–99.9% of the bulk; two hold their limit cycles on finer grids, and their fronts are smooth without any added smoothing. But both have switch-like catalysis, and in a corner the extra impurity triggers runaway growth that the 2D solver can't resolve, so the corner isn't settled ([ADR 0030](adr/0030-h1-with-weak-silica-depletion.md)) | done (corner open) |
| **M18: Model polish** | Eyes cut through the side of the dome now count as eyes (owner approved): random cuts through eye-3d pass 78%, up from 30%, with a new negative control ([ADR 0031](adr/0031-eyes-as-arcs-on-cuts.md)). The 2D front solver handles fast fronts (thin boundary layers), is free of a thread race, steps the coverage implicitly, and stops loudly on failure; it settles ADR 0030's corner: the switch-like set's corner runs ahead and its fast state ends the banding ([ADR 0032](adr/0032-a-front-solver-for-fast-fronts.md)). 3D H2 uses a 19-point, isotropic Laplacian: the spots no longer follow the lattice, though fewer bands form; H2 can take the wall stages of the staged model, and floor-then-rings passes the combined form ([ADR 0033](adr/0033-isotropic-h2-in-3d-and-h2-stages.md)). A re-tune for the band count was tried and closed: the precipitate forms spots a lattice cell across, which no threshold removes (ADR 0033 amendment) | done |
| **The corner question, closed** | Owner decision: within the models tested, fortification's corners point to wall growth the whole wall sees equally (a well-mixed cavity, or reaction-limited growth); the diffusion-limited mechanisms tested don't produce it. No further corner simulations unless asked; the solver scripts are frozen ([ADR 0034](adr/0034-closing-the-corner-question.md)) | closed |
| **M19: The mechanistic tier** | A rule for what a mechanistic verification must show (nothing judged is imposed; conditions may be scheduled, forms may not; every process sourced) and an audit of the eight pending forms: moss, quartz-centre geode, botryoidal, eye and tube look reachable with existing mechanisms; shadow, iris and plume need new sourced physics ([ADR 0035](adr/0035-what-the-mechanistic-tier-requires.md)). Moss: a dendrite stage in the staged model, enclosed by H1 ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-mechanistic-moss.md)). Quartz-centre geode: H1 in a closed cavity runs its silica down into quartz, whose crystals coarsen inward by geometric selection, with nothing scheduled ([ADR 0037](adr/0037-mechanistic-quartz-geode.md)); a closed cavity ends H1's banding after a few bands. Botryoidal: H1 radiating from nucleation sites, growth stopping when the supply ends ([ADR 0038](adr/0038-mechanistic-botryoidal.md)). Eye: a brief spherulite stage overgrown by lining; tube: H1 lining grown from filaments ([ADR 0039](adr/0039-mechanistic-eye-and-tube.md)). Mechanistic tier: 9 of 12; shadow, iris and plume remain. A closed cavity stops H1's banding (silica falls and impurity concentrates); a search of 476 parameter sets found none that keeps visible bands (ADR 0037 amendment). Botryoidal, eye, tube and moss in 3D at the mechanistic tier, on exact solids: random cuts pass 100%, 85%, 13% and 100% ([ADR 0040](adr/0040-mechanistic-forms-in-3d.md)). Reading for the pending forms: milky bands are drier and denser; iris gratings measure about 1.7–64 µm, wider than the iris test's 0.5–5 µm, so the test now asks for 1–65 µm ([ADR 0042](adr/0042-iris-spacing-from-measured-gratings.md), owner approved); a fast second rhythm of the same feedback at a thinner boundary layer lays the iris grating in clear bands, spacing following the growth rate, and passes the iris tests, though iris can't be verified while shadow is pending ([ADR 0043](adr/0043-a-fast-rhythm-for-iris-agate.md)). Plume as a silica garden: chemical-garden tubes rising by buoyancy from seeds on the floor, branching at membrane ruptures, enclosed by H1; mechanistic tier 10 of 12 ([ADR 0044](adr/0044-plume-agate-as-a-silica-garden.md)). Iris no longer depends on shadow (owner approved), so the fast rhythm verifies iris: 11 of 12, shadow the one left ([ADR 0045](adr/0045-iris-no-longer-depends-on-shadow.md)); plume as a chemical garden has no growth model yet ([ADR 0041](adr/0041-reading-for-shadow-iris-and-plume.md)). A geode that bands while open: resupplied until 60% full, then closed (a scheduled change of conditions), the mechanistic geode grows 12 bands instead of 2, a clear zone after closing, and a quartz centre that still coarsens inward ([ADR 0046](adr/0046-a-geode-that-bands-while-open.md)). Plume and the geode in 3D at the mechanistic tier: chemical-garden tubes in 3D enclosed by H1, and crystals in 3D H1's quartz; random cuts pass 79% and 77% (procedural: 68% and 76%), so all ten forms that run in 3D now do so mechanistically ([ADR 0047](adr/0047-plume-and-geode-in-3d.md)). Shadow by a stated link (owner decision): each cycle's impurity-rich half milky, its impurity-poor half clear, for every H1 result; mechanistic tier 12 of 12, shadow resting on an unsourced assumption ([ADR 0048](adr/0048-shadow-agate-by-a-stated-milkiness-link.md)) | done |

| **M25: Finer dendrites** | Owner request: a finer, GPU-based 3D dendrite model, with a middle ground on compute. A study at three lattice spacings found the cell is the finest branch width, not a numerical step, and that the 3D budget had coarsened it from the intended 0.12 mm to about 0.23 mm, lowering the dimension at millimetre scales; the length law matches Hou et al. at either width when seeds are 1 mm apart (amended over four seeds) ([ADR 0056](adr/0056-dendrite-lattice-spacing-is-branch-width.md)). A dendrite engine whose random numbers are hashes of particle and sweep gives the same forest on the CPU and the GPU (8 times faster at 4 million cells); a lattice budget setting (default 4 million cells, as before) and a `moss-fine-3d` preset at 0.12 mm (39 s on the GPU, 2 minutes on the CPU; the 3D view now builds in 21 s after ADR 0059's search-tree fix), whose dimension moves towards Hou et al.'s; moss and plume surveys unchanged within noise ([ADR 0057](adr/0057-a-reproducible-gpu-dendrite-engine.md)) | done |
| **M26: Dendrite lengths in a cavity** | Owner request: moss with closer seeds, to test Hou et al.'s length law in a whole nodule. From a flat surface into open space the model gives it (α 1.1–1.3 at any box height: one tree runs ahead and screens the rest); in a closed nodule no tree can, the cavity cuts the longest off, and closer seeds bring α down only to about 1.7 (seeds 1 mm apart). The model so predicts that dendrites in closed agate cavities don't follow the fracture-surface law. Preset `moss-dense-3d` ([ADR 0058](adr/0058-dendrite-lengths-in-a-closed-cavity.md)) | done |
| **M27: Robustness and caution** | Owner request: bring everything up to date, tidy loose ends and find problems before users do.
